WORKED ANSWERS / FOLLOW THE ARGUMENT
Finite ratios, exact decisions
These exercises explain a published source operation. They are not a new proof run or an independent review.
1. The six retained rows
(1,1), (2,1), (3,2), (5,3), (8,5), (13,8).
Their first/second ratios are 1/1, 2/1, 3/2, 5/3, 8/5 and 13/8.
2. Row count and update count
Depth one returns just [(1,1)]. Depth six performs five updates and retains six rows. In the source, the
loop starts at 1 and stops before depth. The native history index counts updates; the original positive
depth counts rows.
3. Excluding every positive rational fixed ratio
Assume p and q are positive and coprime, and p^2 = pq + q^2. Since p^2 = q(p+q), q divides p^2. If q were
greater than 1, it would have a prime divisor r. Then r would divide p^2, hence p, contradicting coprimality
of p and q. Therefore q = 1.
Also q^2 = p^2 - pq = p(p-q), so p divides q^2. With q = 1, the positive integer p divides 1, forcing p = 1. But
(p,q) = (1,1) makes the assumed equation read 1 = 2. This is a contradiction.
What has been established? No positive rational ratio is an exact fixed point of this update. This
argument quantifies over all coprime positive pairs; it is not a test of only the six rows. Establishing
convergence is a different question from this fixed-point exclusion.
