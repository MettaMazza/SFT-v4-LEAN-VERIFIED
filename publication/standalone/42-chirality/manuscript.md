## Abstract

A complete oriented molecular carrier is chiral exactly when every identity-preserving proper map to its constructed reflection fails. The finite map census is proved complete for arbitrary nonempty distinct occurrence support. Reflection is an involution preserving composition and connectivity, and the non-superposition decision is invariant under reversal of the reflected pair. Appending a fresh occurrence with a unique new elemental label preserves chirality: element preservation forces the new occurrence to map to itself, and every successful extended comparison would restrict to a forbidden original comparison. This paper gives the reflection, enumeration, restriction and closure arguments explicitly, together with the two complete 256-candidate source grammars and the sealed source-comparison protocol. Twelve native Lean modules supply 360 audited definitions and theorems with empty axiom dependencies. Executable reproduction checks 1,352 two-occurrence reflection comparisons and 4,056 uniquely labelled branch extensions against the original canonicalization operations. Observation records retain environment, method, conditions, source and both responses alongside the structural pair.

## 1. Exact carriers and reflection

The original `OrientedStructure` constructor retains a composition word \(C\) of positive counts, an adjacency word \(A\) of positive-count pairs, and a held label \(o\). Its exact admission condition is

\[
 |C|>0\ \land\ |A|>0\ \land\
 \operatorname{family}(o)=\text{molecular-orientation}.
\]

Repeated composition and adjacency entries are retained. There is no added endpoint-range condition in this constructor. `Chirality.admit`, `emptyCompositionRejected`, `emptyAdjacencyRejected` and `nonemptyAccepted` express those original decisions. The original witness has composition \((2,1)\), adjacency \(((1,2))\) and label `molecular-orientation:right`; `originalRawAccepted` and `originalWitnesses` establish its admission and the three registered operational checks [4,6].

The molecular comparison uses the existing named molecular carrier

\[
 M=(N,C,E,Q,O),
\]

where \(N\) is the ordered occurrence-name word, \(C\) its element-label word, \(E\) the bond list, \(Q\) the charge-fibre record and \(O\) the named orientation records. Molecular admission checks equal positive name/element widths, distinct nonblank occurrence names, retained element identities, registered distinct bond endpoints, no repeated normalized bond, connectivity and no open charge fibre. These are the source's molecular-domain checks, preserved by `MolecularCarrier.admit`. The oriented raw constructor and the named molecular admission have their own exact input records [5].

An orientation cell is structural EmptyOne or a held label. The two Fold fibre labels are \(f_1\) and \(f_2\). Reflection preserves the family and exchanges the labels:

\[
 r(\mathrm{EmptyOne})=\mathrm{EmptyOne},\qquad
 r(f_1)=f_2,\qquad r(f_2)=f_1.
\]

The total label operation leaves other labels unchanged. In the original admitted coordination forms, every occurrence carries a nonempty three-axis word over precisely EmptyOne, \(f_1\) and \(f_2\). Thus there are \(3^3-1=26\) admitted words. For \(w=(w_1,w_2,w_3)\), put

\[
 Rw=(r(w_1),r(w_2),r(w_3)).
\]

Case analysis on the cell establishes \(r(r(x))=x\): EmptyOne is fixed, the first fibre passes through the second and back, the second passes through the first and back, and any other retained label is fixed. Applying this result to all three components yields \(R(Rw)=w\). These are `reflectLabelTwice`, `reflectCellTwice` and `reflectWordTwice`.

The molecular reflection is constructed by

\[
 RM=(N,C,E,Q,[(a,Rw):(a,w)\in O]).
\]

It retains all names, elements, bonds and charge records, and transforms every orientation record in place. Induction over \(O\) proves reflection twice recovers the full record list; hence `mirrorMoleculeTwice` gives \(R(RM)=M\). Lookup is the first matching named row. Induction over that list shows

\[
 O_{RM}(a)=\operatorname{map}(R,O_M(a)),
\]

where lookup returns an optional word. Missing rows remain missing. The proper comparison below requires both corresponding words to be present. Reflection changes no source ordering or duplicate-row decision.

## 2. Exhaustive maps and exact proper comparison

For nonempty distinct occurrence word \(N\), let \(\mathcal P(N)\) be the list generated by `arrangementsCore`. At a singleton \([a]\), its sole order is \([a]\). At larger support, generation chooses every possible leading occurrence \(a\in N\), removes that occurrence, recursively generates every remaining order and prepends \(a\):

\[
 \mathcal P(N)=\mathop{\mathrm{concat}}_{a\in N}
 [a::q:q\in\mathcal P(N\setminus a)].
\]

The removed word is strictly shorter, so this recursion terminates. Distinctness makes removal exact. Every generated order permutes the carrier: the recursive suffix permutes the remainder and the leading occurrence restores the removed member. Conversely, take any permutation \(a::q\) of \(N\). Its head belongs to \(N\); removal preserves permutation, so \(q\) permutes \(N\setminus a\). Length induction places \(q\) in the recursive branch and therefore \(a::q\) in the full list. `everyArrangementPermutesCarrier`, `suffixPermutesRemainder` and `everyPermutationGenerated` establish

\[
 p\in\mathcal P(N)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Perm}(p,N).
\]

For any supplied Boolean comparison \(b\), retain the full trace

\[
 T(N,b)=[(p,b(p)):p\in\mathcal P(N)].
\]

A true `any` result has a witnessed generated order; the correspondence above turns it into a permutation. A permutation with true comparison occurs in the generated list and therefore forces `any` to be true. Negating these two directions gives

\[
 \operatorname{any}_{p\in\mathcal P(N)}b(p)=\mathrm{false}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall p,\ \operatorname{Perm}(p,N)\Rightarrow b(p)=\mathrm{false}.
\]

`exhaustiveSuperposition` and `noSuperpositionExactly` prove these statements for the complete quantified domain, with every failed comparison retained in `mapTrace`.

For molecular carriers \(M,M'\), an order \(p\) of the target names defines the positionwise map \(\phi_p\) by zipping the source occurrence word with \(p\). Normalize each undirected bond by endpoint order and retain its set. The executable proper comparison \(P(M,M',p)\) is the conjunction of three requirements:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &C_M(a)=C_{M'}(\phi_p(a))&&\text{for every source occurrence }a,\\
 &\phi_p(E_M)\subseteq E_{M'}\quad\land\quad
 E_{M'}\subseteq\phi_p(E_M),\\
 &O_M(a)=O_{M'}(\phi_p(a))&&\text{with both orientation words present.}
 \end{aligned}
\]

`MolecularIsomer.candidateMeaning` derives the element requirement and both bond-inclusion directions. `properMatchRetainsConstitution` and `properMatchRetainsOrientation` extract the complete obligations from a true `properMatches`. Word equality is componentwise equality of the three held cells. The superposition operation also checks equality of composition signatures before the complete target-order search.

For \(M' = RM\), the composition signatures are identical by construction. Therefore the chirality decision \(\chi(M)=\neg\operatorname{hasProperSuperposition}(M,RM)\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\quad\chi(M)=\mathrm{true}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall p\in\operatorname{Perm}(N),\ P(M,RM,p)=\mathrm{false}.\quad}
\]

This is `chiralityExactlyAllProperMapsFail`, for every nonempty distinct occurrence support. Its proof substitutes the preserved composition signature and applies the two enumeration directions. All element, bond and orientation checks are made by the concrete comparison; the census does not choose a preferred map.

## 3. The base pair and canonical source comparison

The native base has two occurrences \(a,b\), distinct elemental labels `first` and `second`, one bond \((a,b)\), and word \((f_1,\mathrm{EmptyOne},\mathrm{EmptyOne})\) at each occurrence. Reflection replaces each \(f_1\) by \(f_2\). There are exactly two orders:

\[
 \mathcal P([a,b])=[[a,b],[b,a]].
\]

The identity order preserves the elements and bond but fails the reflected orientation comparison. The swapped order fails the distinct elemental identities. Both proper maps therefore fail. `baseOrders`, `baseChiral` and `baseEveryProperMapFails` give the complete base derivation.

The pinned coordination operation `_canonical` generates every occurrence permutation, serializes the composition label, attachment label and complete orientation word of every reordered occurrence, and serializes the normalized reordered bond set. It selects the least resulting complete signature. Two forms have equal canonical signatures exactly when their permutation orbits contain the same complete serialization. Equality supplies the corresponding relabelling by composing the two witnessing permutations; a preserving relabelling makes the two permutation orbits identical and therefore gives equal minima. `_mirror` implements the same fibre exchange, retaining all composition labels, attachments and adjacency pairs.

The source comparisons use held equal attachment labels on the molecular carrier. All \(26^2\) ordered pairs of nonempty orientation words and both equal/unequal elemental-label cases give

\[
 2\cdot26^2=1352
\]

two-occurrence comparisons. Exhaustive proper-map comparison agrees with `_canonical(F) != _canonical(_mirror(F))` throughout: 1,326 are chiral and 26 are achiral. For the achiral cases, the equal-label exchange pairs a word with its reflected partner and preserves the single bond. The retained native comparison evidence is TURN624; the portable source replay reproduces these counts.

## 4. Uniquely labelled extension preserves chirality

Let \(x\notin N\) be a fresh occurrence name and \(e\notin C\) a fresh elemental label. A branch with orientation \(w\) and supplied links \(L\) is appended as

\[
 M^+=(N\mathbin{+\!+}[x],C\mathbin{+\!+}[e],
 E\mathbin{+\!+}[(a,x):a\in L],Q,O\mathbin{+\!+}[(x,w)]).
\]

This is `appendMolecularBranch`; for one link, `singleBranchUsesExistingAppend` identifies its carrier with the original `MolecularCarrier.appendOccurrence`. Old records remain in their original positions. Reflection distributes over the orientation-list append, giving

\[
 R(M\oplus(x,e,w,L))=RM\oplus(x,e,Rw,L).
\]

`molecularBranchReflection` proves this equation. The source coordination successor retains its composition, attachment and orientation words and all old adjacency pairs in the same manner.

### 4.1 The extended census covers every old map

Deleting fresh \(x\) from any permutation of \(N\mathbin{+\!+}[x]\) leaves a permutation of \(N\). This follows by induction on permutation construction: deletion commutes with a preserved leading occurrence, and a swap or composition preserves the reduced permutation. Conversely, for each old order \(q\), the order \(x::q\) permutes the extended carrier. Enumeration completeness puts it in the extended generated census. `everyExtendedMapRestricts`, `everyGeneratedExtensionRestrictsToOldCensus` and `everyOldMapHasGeneratedExtension` prove both coverage directions.

### 4.2 An accepted map must fix the new occurrence

Consider two extended carriers of equal old width with fresh occurrence \(x\), new element \(e\), and an accepted proper map \(p\). Element preservation at \(x\) says

\[
 e=C_{M'^+}(\phi_p(x)).
\]

Permutation membership places \(\phi_p(x)\) in the old target carrier or at \(x\). In the old carrier its element belongs to the old element word, contradicting freshness of \(e\). Thus

\[
 \phi_p(x)=x.
\]

This is `uniqueBranchLabelForcesFixedName`. It uses actual zipped element lookup and equal name/element widths. Because \(x\) is the last source occurrence and absent from the preceding word, induction through that zip proves \(p=q\mathbin{+\!+}[x]\). `fixedLastMappingSplits` gives this decomposition; `fixedBranchMapRestrictsPermutation` removes the fresh final entry and proves \(q\) permutes the old target carrier. Fixing the new occurrence is a consequence of the complete comparison, not a restriction on the enumerated candidates.

### 4.3 All original comparisons survive restriction

For each old occurrence \(a\), lookup in the extended zipped map agrees with lookup in \(q\); the appended row cannot replace an earlier old name. The same induction preserves old element and orientation lookups. Thus a successful extended comparison supplies every old element and orientation comparison (`alignedBranchElementsRestrict`, `alignedBranchOrientationRestricts`).

For bonds, every added edge contains \(x\). Every old edge avoids \(x\), because old endpoints belong to the old carrier and \(x\) is fresh. Its image under the old permutation also avoids \(x\). Consequently, an old mapped edge belonging to the extended target edge set must belong to the old target edge set. In the reverse direction, an old target edge belonging to the extended mapped set cannot come from an added edge: that edge's image contains the fixed occurrence \(x\). It therefore comes from an old edge. Normalization preserves this endpoint distinction. Hence both inclusions restrict:

\[
 \phi_q(E_M)\subseteq E_{M'},\qquad
 E_{M'}\subseteq\phi_q(E_M).
\]

`branchEdgeSetRestricts`, `alignedBranchForwardBondsRestrict` and `alignedBranchReverseBondsRestrict` provide these implications. Combining the element, bond and orientation restrictions gives

\[
 P(M^+,M'^+,p)=\mathrm{true}
 \Rightarrow\exists q\in\operatorname{Perm}(N'),\ P(M,M',q)=\mathrm{true}.
\]

This is `uniqueBranchProperRestriction`. Its hypotheses are fresh occurrence names on both sides, fresh elemental label in the old target, equal old name widths, name/element width agreement, distinct target names and complete old bond-endpoint coverage. It retains the supplied orientation words and branch links.

### 4.4 Closure follows by contradiction

Set \(M'=RM\). Reflection supplies equal carriers and endpoint data. If \(M\) is chiral, Section 2 says every old proper map fails. A successful map of \(M^+\) to its reflection would restrict to a successful old map, contradicting that result. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\chi(M)=\mathrm{true}\Rightarrow
 \chi(M\oplus(x,e,w,L))=\mathrm{true}.}
\]

`uniqueBranchPreservesChirality` quantifies over nonempty distinct old occurrence names, matching name/element widths, covered old bond endpoints, fresh \(x\), fresh \(e\), and original chirality. It accepts every supplied orientation word and link list. For an admitted molecular carrier, `admittedMoleculeShape` derives the name and width conditions, and `allConstituentEndpoints` derives endpoint coverage. `admittedUniqueBranchPreservesChirality` then binds closure directly to the admitted source carrier.

The retained TURN625 execution compares all 1,352 original two-occurrence cases after appending a third occurrence with a unique elemental label and fixed nonempty orientation, linked to the first, second or both original occurrences. All \(1352\cdot3=4056\) source/native results agree and are chiral. The general restriction argument above applies to arbitrary finite admitted carriers; the executable census provides the complete retained source comparison for these branch inputs.

## 5. Enantiomer pairs and symmetric exclusion

`MirrorPair` retains both complete records and the exact relation \(M'=RM\). `generate` constructs this relation. Reflection preserves the underlying carrier, so `completeConstitution`, `equalComposition` and `equalConnectivity` establish equality of all carrier fields, elements and bonds. `opposedConfiguration` gives the reflected optional orientation lookup at every name. Reflection twice yields \(M=RM'\), constructing the reversed pair.

Classification is \(\operatorname{classify}(M,RM)=\chi(M)\). The complete-map equivalence of Section 2 therefore gives

\[
 \operatorname{classify}(M,RM)=\mathrm{true}
 \Longleftrightarrow\forall p\in\operatorname{Perm}(N),\ P(M,RM,p)=\mathrm{false}.
\]

`comparisonTrace` retains every generated order with its actual comparison. For orientation words, involution proves

\[
 Ra=b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a=Rb.
\]

Reflect the first equality and use \(R^2=\mathrm{id}\) for the forward direction; reflect the second for the reverse. `wordReflectionTransfer` turns this into equality of executable word-comparison results. The optional version checks the absent cases explicitly: every comparison requiring a missing word is false on both sides. Induction over the occurrence list transfers reflection through every orientation comparison. Constitution is unchanged, so

\[
 P(RM,M',p)=P(M,RM',p).
\]

Putting \(M'=M\) proves `pairedTestsSymmetric`:

\[
 P(RM,M,p)=P(M,RM,p).
\]

Both members have the same occurrence carrier, so every failed comparison for the first ordering is a failed comparison for the reverse ordering. `classifiedPairSymmetric` proves reversal preserves classification. `basePairClassified` instantiates the complete base from Section 3.

For matched extension, `appendMatched` constructs

\[
 (M\oplus(x,e,w,L),\ RM\oplus(x,e,Rw,L)).
\]

Reflection/append commutation proves its exact mirror relation. `matchedExtensionConstitution` preserves the complete shared constitution. `matchedExtensionEveryOldMap` supplies an extended generated order for every old permutation. With the unique-label hypotheses of Section 4, the chirality closure theorem supplies the classified extended pair.

## 7. Complete source grammars

Each source entry declares eight independent binary coordinates. The generator takes their literal Cartesian product, in the registered order, giving \(2^8=256\) candidates. Each coordinate retains both its rejected and admitted alternative and the original elimination reason. The first failed coordinate supplies the rejection reason.

### 7.1 Chirality

| Coordinate | Rejected alternative | Admitted alternative |
|---|---|---|
| carrier | formula only | complete three dimensional object |
| reflection | mirror image not generated | exact mirror image generated |
| composition | mirror composition changed | composition preserved |
| adjacency | mirror adjacency changed | adjacency preserved |
| superposition | visual guess | all proper finite relabelings enumerated |
| result | arbitrary handedness name | no proper superposition |
| record | chirality label only | orientation and map trace |
| extra | free chirality rule | no extra rule |


### 7.2 Enantiomer pair

| Coordinate | Rejected alternative | Admitted alternative |
|---|---|---|
| members | one structure only | paired stereoisomers |
| constitution | different connectivity | same composition and connectivity |
| mirror | unrelated orientations | exact mirror relation |
| superposition | superposable pair | non superposable pair |
| orientation | orientation erased | opposed configurations held |
| environment | universal property difference | chiral observation boundary |
| record | pair name only | paired map and observation trace |
| extra | free enantiomer rule | no extra rule |


For a candidate \(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_8)\), the criteria decision is the conjunction that each coordinate takes its admitted alternative:

\[
 D(c)=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{8}[c_i=\mathrm{admitted}_i].
\]

Membership of each binary coordinate in its two-element list, followed by eight nested product-membership steps, proves every candidate occurs. `candidatesComplete` carries out those steps; `generatedCardinality` reduces the full list to length 256. Exhaustive case analysis of the eight constructor choices proves the source identifier decision equals \(D(c)\) in `sourceDecisionMatchesCriteria`.

Let \(c_*\) be the all-admitted candidate. If \(D(c)\) holds, each component equals the corresponding component of \(c_*\), so \(c=c_*\). Conversely, substituting \(c_*\) makes every comparison true. `uniqueCriteriaSurvivor`, `uniqueSurvivor` and `acceptanceIffExactCriteria` prove these implications. Every rejected-axis theorem shows that its rejected alternative forces the whole conjunction false. Thus the complete registered grammar has exactly one survivor. Sections 1–6 provide the explicit carrier, exhaustive-map, reflection, branch and observation constructions retained by that survivor.
