ONE QUESTION TO FOLLOW
When a calculator displays an enclosure, what determines which endpoint lies above the result?

For 0 < x <= 1, let
A_N = sum over j = 0,...,N-1 of (-1)^j x^(2j+1)/(2j+1),
q_N = x^(2N+1)/(2N+1).
The paper's corrected arctangent operation returns:
  odd N:  [A_N - q_N, A_N]
  even N: [A_N, A_N + q_N].
The next term's sign determines the side of the partial sum on which the result lies. Both endpoints are rational.

TRY TWO TERM COUNTS
Set x = 1/5.
For N = 1, A_1 = 1/5 and q_1 = 1/375, giving
  [74/375, 1/5].
For N = 2, A_2 = 74/375 and q_2 = 1/15625, giving
  [74/375, 9253/46875].
These are arithmetic illustrations of the published branches. They are not additional completion receipts or new Lean results.

FOLLOW THE IMPLEMENTATION
In the release's source archive, open:
  v4/SFT/Mathematics/CalculatorOperationsV2.lean
The full declaration is:
  SFT.Mathematics.CalculatorOperationsV2.atanUnitEnclosureV2
Read its guards (positive x at most One; positive term count), construction of positive and counter sums, next-term calculation, parity branch and returned certificate.

Then read:
  SFT.Mathematics.CalculatorOperationsV2.circleConstantEnclosure
It combines enclosures for atan(1/5) and atan(1/239) using
  16*atan(1/5) - 4*atan(1/239).
If the two intervals are [a,b] and [c,d], the resulting endpoints are
  [16a - 4d, 16b - 4c].
Section 5 supplies the angle-composition derivation as well as the enclosure operation.

FOLLOW THE RESULT INTO THE INTERFACE
Sections 1–2 explain why decimal input 0.1+0.2 becomes exactly 3/10. Sections 6–7 follow parser precedence, decimal projection and transactional admission. Section 8 identifies the native source and retained evaluator/controller/browser comparisons. Distinguish the exact value and certificate from its decimal display, and distinguish a mathematical argument from a particular recorded execution.
