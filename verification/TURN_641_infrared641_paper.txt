
Infrared recurrence and molecular distinction

The retained molecule consists of its admitted bonded carrier and complete relative-orientation record. Each internal state retains its recurrence, displacement record, interaction trace and source record. A transition retains both molecular states, the incident recurrence, counted response and provenance. Its endpoints belong to the declared molecular-state support. This preserves the original source's paired-transition domain and observation boundary.

Let R be the complete ordered list of registered transitions. For a transition t, write d_-(t) and d_+(t) for its predecessor and successor interaction traces. The coupling decision is exact:
    c(t) = true if and only if d_-(t) ≠ d_+(t).
The visible support and retained non-coupled support are
    V(R) = [t in R | d_-(t) ≠ d_+(t)],
    U(R) = [t in R | d_-(t) = d_+(t)].
Equality decidability gives the two exhaustive cases for every registered transition. Their supports are disjoint: membership in both would assert equality and its negation. Thus an unchanged interaction is excluded from visible support but remains recorded in U(R) and in the complete decision trace [(t,c(t)) | t in R].

The incident recurrence is a positive-length return path. Relative to a retained reference recurrence with k completed changes, a transition whose incident path has n completed changes has the exact recurrence frequency
    ν(t) = k/n,  k > 0, n > 0.
If its normalized representation is a/b, the normalization proof yields k b = a n. This construction reuses the existing positive recurrence-ratio derivation. A molecular internal recurrence retains its return equality: its terminal carrier equals its initial carrier.

For counted response q(t), the complete pattern is
    P(R) = [(ν(t),q(t)) | t in V(R)].
The forward correspondence proof shows that every coupled registered transition contributes its pair. The reverse proof shows that every pattern pair comes from such a transition. No isolated line substitutes for the complete pattern: the comparison evaluates equality of the full ordered list of normalized frequency numerators, denominators and counted responses. Its Boolean result is proved equivalent to equality of those complete signatures; it does not assert that one shared line determines molecular identity.

The base contains one predecessor/successor pair. When its interaction changes, filtering the singleton retains that row and mapping yields exactly [(ν(t),q(t))]. Extending the state support by s and retaining the full appended transition list A gives
    V(R ++ A) = V(R) ++ V(A),
    U(R ++ A) = U(R) ++ U(A),
    P(R ++ A) = P(R) ++ [(ν(t),q(t)) | t in V(A)].
These equations follow by induction on the earlier transition list and preservation of ordered mapping over concatenation. Every supplied newly coupled transition is retained; prior visible and non-coupled records are preserved. Each appended transition's endpoints remain bound to the extended state support. The method and conditions remain unchanged.
