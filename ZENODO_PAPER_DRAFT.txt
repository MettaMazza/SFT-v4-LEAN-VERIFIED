Smithian Fold Theory: A Lean Formalisation of the V3 Model

Maria Smith
Working manuscript — unpublished

Abstract

We present a native Lean formalisation of Smithian Fold Theory V3, preserving its definitions, dependency order, declared domains and zero-axiom, zero-free-parameter constraints. The formalisation begins with the operational root, There Is No Nothing, followed by the structural One and exact positive finite count. At the scope established here, their registered candidate grammars contain two, six and ten classes respectively. Constructive proofs establish exhaustive enumeration, the unique surviving class and acceptance of that survivor for each grammar. The 450 audited proofs have no axiom dependencies. The translated classifications agree with the V3 artifacts on every candidate's order, identifier, exact form, decision and reason.

1. Model and scope

The source model is Smithian Fold Theory V3, repository MettaMazza/ernos-labs-sft-platform, commit 76f1b34e9e9682989adcbb088797b4ae4ce4fcf1. Its census registers 2,781 model-admitted claims. The complete formalisation follows all 2,781 entries in dependency order, preserving their original definitions, derivations, declared constraints, controls and empirical boundaries. Each translated component is bound to its V3 source, and full-model verification requires both native proofs of the registered formal statements and correspondence of the associated executable procedures. Empirical records retain their original evidential status. The results presented here concern eight foundation claims, including finite-generation traces and the exact positive operations. Full-entry verification remains distinct from the translated portions.

SFT distinguishes the structural One, generated positive finite counts and exact positive parts from host implementation indices. Its derivational constraints exclude added axioms, free or fitted scientific parameters, negative proof quantities, irrational or imaginary proof values, floating equality and completed infinity. Formalisation preserves the registered source boundary of each result.

2. Operational root

The registered operational challenge partition has exactly two classes: unpresented-absence and presented-occurrence. An unpresented challenge supplies no counterexample. A presented challenge supplies an occurrence and is therefore not nothing at the operational boundary.

The decision accepts precisely presented-occurrence. Exhaustive enumeration establishes that both registered classes occur in the candidate list. The unique-survivor theorem establishes that a candidate is accepted if and only if it is presented-occurrence. No premise about a particular presentation's internal content is introduced.

Within the registered partition, deleting the presented witness leaves no accepted candidate. The decision is definitionally the presentation predicate. Three additional native proofs establish the false-premise, tampered-artifact and fixed-boundary control outcomes. The source census, decisions, closure evidence and all four control records are emitted by native Lean definitions; every stored field and digest agrees with the pinned V3 artifacts.

The tampered-artifact premise is represented by an acceptance function that accepts both source classes. Its survivor set is exactly the original two-element candidate list. Constructive proofs establish that the two survivors are distinct and that no unique survivor exists. These statements formalise the source rejection condition while retaining the original control record for exact artifact correspondence.

The record-level formalisation retains exactly candidate identity, presentation flag and descriptive form from the source dictionary. Classification is derived from the presentation flag. For every record in this schema, its classified candidate has the same presentation status; classifying a generated representative returns that representative. Two records have the same class if and only if their presentation flags agree. Thus every such record maps into the registered exhaustive partition, independently of its identifier or descriptive text.

The source decision agrees with this classification for every record. Acceptance is equivalent to the presented-occurrence class and to the source operational presentation witness; an accepted record cannot be unpresented. Changing either metadata string changes neither acceptance nor its reason. Deleting presentation rejects the record. These statements formalise the source record decision and operational presentation relation, while preserving all three source fields and the generated evidence values.

Source claim: SFT-ROOT-THERE-IS-NO-NOTHING.

3. Structural One

A representation of the root occurrence is classified by its coverage—none, proper or complete—and by whether it adds unforced material. The product gives six classes: omitted-root, replacement-extra, fragmented-root, fragment-plus-extra, exact-self-whole and whole-plus-extra, in source enumeration order.

The representation survives precisely when its coverage is complete and it contains no extra material. Thus exact-self-whole is the unique surviving class. Omission, fragmentation and addition are rejected by the same ordered conditions as in V3. The One is structural self-wholeness, not a numerical magnitude introduced at this stage.

The record-level derivation uses the three source root-coverage relations and two source extra-coverage relations. Their generated product is proved identical, in order, to the six stored candidates. Classification retains both relations and is inverted by the generated representatives. The four source record fields, survival decision and ordered reason all agree with the generated records.

For every record in that relation domain, acceptance is equivalent to complete coverage together with no extra material, and hence to the exact-self-whole class. Replacing coverage by none or proper rejects the record; setting the extra relation to has-extra also rejects it. Acceptance and its reason are unchanged by arbitrary identifier or descriptive-text changes. These universal record statements formalise the source omission, fragmentation, addition and metadata-independence arguments without introducing a numerical magnitude.

Source claim: SFT-FOUNDATION-ONE-001. Registered dependency: the operational root.

4. Exact positive finite count

V3 defines positive finite count through the complete generation trace of a nonempty finite succession of structural-One occurrences. Every generated occurrence is retained once and no ungenerated occurrence is added.

Its representation grammar has ten classes. With no coverage, multiplicity is not applicable and the two extra-material classes remain. With proper or complete coverage, multiplicity is once or duplicated, independently paired with absent or present extra material. Only complete-coverage__once__no-extra survives. The formal classification retains this exact domain, including the absence of a multiplicity choice in the no-coverage case.

The native theorems establish complete enumeration and unique survival within this registered grammar. No replacement numerical count model is required for these results.

The base/successor argument is encoded as finite generation syntax: a structural-One base followed by extensions, each retaining prior occurrence positions and introducing a fresh terminal. Positions denote generated occurrence identities within the trace. They are not interpreted as a numerical magnitude. The complete representation is constructed by retaining the base and appending each newly generated terminal once.

Structural induction proves that every generated position occurs in the complete trace, that no position is repeated, and that the trace is nonempty. The previous complete trace is proper coverage of an extension: all its positions remain, but the fresh terminal is absent. Appending that terminal restores completeness. Repeating any generated position violates exact multiplicity; removing a position loses complete coverage. The two source complete-generation clauses determine a unique trace identity at every finite extension. Finite proposed representations are then classified by their retained generated positions and presence of ungenerated material. The latter is represented by the source outside-trace relation; extra-object content is not a criterion of the source classifier.

Constructive position equality and membership establish the semantic connection to the ten-class grammar. Complete coverage is equivalent to retaining every generated position. The multiplicity predicate is equivalent to absence of repeated retained positions. The extra-material predicate is equivalent to the presence of an ungenerated occurrence. For every finite proposal, survival is therefore equivalent to complete exact-once retention with no ungenerated material. Canonical complete proposals survive; any missing position, repeated retained position or outside addition is rejected. The previous complete prefix of an extension is rejected because it omits the new terminal.

Execution comparisons cover 493 proposals over one-, two- and three-occurrence traces, reaching all ten source classes and matching their decisions and reasons. These bounded comparisons supplement the universal Lean equivalence; they do not replace it.

Source claim: SFT-FOUNDATION-COUNT-001. Registered dependency: the structural One.

4.1 Exact positive part coordinate

The Part derivation registers a finite partition of the structural One and a held selection. Its seven relation domains classify partition coverage, overlap, fibre equality, selection relation, held-count presence, whole-count presence and unforced-extra-coordinate presence. Their source-ordered product contains 192 forms. A native theorem establishes that acceptance is equivalent to all seven requirements: complete, disjoint, equal-fibred, within-partition, both counts present and no extra coordinate. Exactly that form survives. Removing either count, omitting whole coverage, allowing overlap or unequal fibres, using an empty or outside selection, or adding an unforced coordinate is proved to fail.

For a registered whole’s finite generated fibre trace, a held predicate constructs the selected trace. Constructive proofs establish containment and absence of repeated fibres. A witnessed held fibre makes the selection nonempty. Its host-record length cannot exceed the whole trace length; this expresses the finite containment bound without installing a new magnitude in the model. The exact coordinate is the pair of held and whole generation records. Its projections retain those two identities, and the pair is unique from them. Both records are nonempty for a witnessed selection.

Source claim: SFT-FOUNDATION-PART-001. Registered dependencies: structural One and Count. Equivalence of coordinates from different partitions is not assumed here; it is the subject of the separately registered common-refinement derivation.

The common-refinement construction generates cells by traversing each source A label and then each source B label. The selected A lift retains all B descendants of each held A label; the selected B lift retains all A origins of each held B label. Every generated cell has source origins, and each pair of source origins occurs in the construction. The executable label formatting and order are preserved exactly.

The registered generated-bijection procedure pairs finite host indices in order and checks equal trace lengths and bounds. Native proofs establish that the bounds always hold, that acceptance is equivalent to equal finite trace lengths, and that every index on both accepted sides occurs in the generated pairing. A constructive pairing supplies forward and inverse maps with both inverse identities; injectivity and onto coverage follow. Identity, reversal and chaining establish the registered pairing laws. Uniform refinement retains the shared descendant identity and preserves both inverse identities. For the concrete pair-cell procedure, equal selected-trace lengths remain equal after appending the same descendant trace by nested generation. This is proved structurally without assuming a multiplication law. The source one-of-two/two-of-four example accepts; the unequal source example rejects.

A constructive product-membership proof establishes exhaustive enumeration. The source identifier-equality decision is proved equivalent to all nine witness requirements; exactly the registered witness survives. Eleven adverse relation cases are proved rejected. Complete selected-origin lifting is proved contained in the common refinement, and the accepted generated pairing is both one-to-one and onto on the lifted trace positions. 4.3 Minimal Count extension in Fold

Fold classifies a generated Count stage as identity, first extension or later extension. Structural proofs identify those classes exactly with the base, its immediate successor and a successor of a successor. The generated-stage prefix relation retains each earlier stage under further extension. Every nonidentity finite trace contains the first extension. The first extension is nonidentity and every nonidentity prefix of it is itself; it is therefore minimal. Conversely, any minimal nonidentity stage must equal that first extension. Identity and every later finite extension are consequently excluded as minimal Fold stages.

The first extension retains the base occurrence and the appended occurrence. Its generated-position domain is exhausted by these two distinct positions, and its complete record retains each once. This formalises the source first-extension count argument without introducing signed numerical labels, a fitted quantity or a maximum finite depth. The remaining Fold obligations concern equal/disjoint whole-preserving fibres, distinct held labels and the explicit return relation.

The seven source relation domains generate 288 forms in the original order. The native decision retains the exact source conjunction and ordered explanations. Exhaustive membership, unique survival and acceptance iff all seven requirements are established. Nine adverse criterion values are rejected. A source-record adapter derives the extension field from the actual Count stage and preserves the other six fields. Its acceptance is equivalent to minimal nonidentity of that stage together with complete whole coverage, disjoint and equal fibres, distinct-held labels, present return and absence of extra data. Identity and every later stage are rejected for every choice of the remaining source fields.

4.4 Complete finite Fold-word assembly and return

The assembly construction begins with the structural empty One word. Each successor appends held-a and held-b, in that order, to every preceding word. The nonnegative extension index is the source host recursion input; the empty word is a structural form. Negative host extension inputs are rejected.

Appending a held label retains both the predecessor word and the last label. The return relation recovers the predecessor by removing precisely the appended terminal label. Both distinct branches return to the same predecessor, and a successor has only one such predecessor. These properties hold at every finite generated depth. Appending different labels or extending different predecessor words produces distinct successor words; induction establishes uniqueness of every complete support.

A word belongs to the support after n extensions if and only if it has host-record length n and every label belongs to the source pair. This universal equivalence establishes completeness and absence of additional words. Every retained successor has a return to the preceding support. An outside label or incorrect length excludes a proposed word; repeating a generated word violates uniqueness, and omitting one violates complete coverage. These statements are established by constructive finite-list and generation induction without an imported exponentiation law or a maximum depth.

Source claim: SFT-FOUNDATION-FOLD-ASSEMBLY-001. Dependencies: Count and Fold. The source keeps this recursive assembly claim separate from minimal Fold. Its seven relation domains produce 192 source-ordered forms. Exhaustive enumeration, equivalence of the original identifier decision with the seven criteria, unique survival and rejection of eight adverse values are established. Complete evidence integration remains outstanding.

The canonical assembly retains every stage from the empty source word through the final finite depth. Each transition explicitly retains predecessor, appended label and successor. Its successor uniquely determines that transition. Every generated child at every earlier level has both its transition and return recorded in the full trace. Conversely, each stored stage, transition and return arises from the source construction; each return recovers its recorded predecessor.

A native proof certificate establishes the seven accepted relation requirements on this canonical construction: complete stage coverage, consistent lengths, complete support, word uniqueness, transition retention, return retention and absence of added records. The accepted source witness is realised at every positive finite depth. This result concerns the canonical construction. Proposed word supports additionally admit the executable semantic classification below; source coverage, transition and return relations remain independent inputs.

For an arbitrary finite proposed word support, executable membership and universal finite-list tests determine the source length, support-completeness, uniqueness and extra-word relations. A native theorem establishes acceptance exactly when the coverage relation is complete, every proposed word has the required length, every generated word is retained, no word is repeated, transitions and returns are present, every proposed word is generated and no extra rule is supplied. The coverage, transition and return fields are preserved as independent source relations; their truth is not inferred from a word list alone.

The source finite-set operations are represented constructively. Duplicate removal preserves precisely the original members and produces a duplicate-free list. Equality of the original length and this finite-set cardinality is equivalent to uniqueness. Two word sets compare equal exactly when their membership predicates agree. The original four assembly controls retain their predicates and serialized result hashes; the depth-one-through-five uniqueness/length check follows from the native all-finite theorem. The source literal boundary text check remains distinct from semantic exclusion results.

The finite assembly record representation includes predecessor–label–successor transitions and child–parent returns. Acceptance is equivalent to retention of every required transition and return, exclusion of ungenerated records, complete source coverage, correct word lengths, complete word support, word uniqueness and absence of extra words or rules. Canonical records satisfy these conditions. Missing required transitions, missing required returns and incorrect returns are rejected. Coverage remains the explicit source relation. Registration, census, decisions and closure are represented with the original source fields and ordering; candidate lookup uses the source identifier.

The exact casting operation acts on a positive count ratio n/d by removing one complete denominator while the numerator exceeds it. A structural recursion bound derived from n suffices for every positive d. A native removal-trace theorem proves termination and a unique retained numerator r with 0 < r ≤ d. Admitted numerators remain fixed; adding a complete denominator to a positive numerator does not change its retained value. Every positive whole number of complete turns retains d, representing the One. These results apply to all finite positive count pairs. The numerator-level result supplies the casting stage of the normalized primitive operations below.

The Fraction admission map preserves the reduced numerator and denominator exactly. An admitted part maps back to the same Fraction, and admission yields that part if and only if the original Fraction is its reduced-pair representation. Zero, signed values and values above the One are rejected; every reduced positive pair through the One is admitted. For admitted a and b, the strict Take guard is the cross-product inequality between their count pairs. Its difference numerator is positive and strictly smaller than the product denominator; equal and reversed Take inputs are rejected. Self-junction followed by cast has a positive numerator no larger than its denominator, and a complete One is Fold-fixed. The normalized operations reduce their output count pairs to the canonical Fraction representation.

Canonical Fraction construction divides the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor. Constructive proofs identify the native GCD computation, establish its divisibility and greatest-common-divisor properties, and show that the reduced quotient pair is coprime. Both quotients are positive, reduction preserves exact cross-product value, and an admitted input remains through the One. Normalization is stable on an already reduced part. The integer-pair constructor retains the source rejection messages for signed, zero and beyond-One inputs.

Fold first reduces the self-junction Fraction, then casts complete Ones and reduces the retained part. Guarded Take constructs and reduces its exact positive difference. Both operations return reduced admitted parts. Unison normalizes the quotient and compares it to the One; a native theorem makes this equivalent to equality of the input cross-products. Every part is in unison with itself, and the complete One is Fold-fixed. The proof arguments apply to all finite positive count pairs and use no denominator bound.

5. Formal representation and results

Each Lean candidate constructor denotes exactly one registered V3 candidate record. These constructors are encodings of the existing finite grammar. Field projections retain source text and field values. Decision functions implement the original branch predicates and rejection order.

For each of the seven grammars, the formalisation proves:

(a) Every candidate belongs to the enumerated domain.
(b) Acceptance is equivalent to equality with the source survivor.
(c) The source survivor is accepted.

The proofs use constructor elimination, reflexivity and explicit list membership. Lean reports no axiom dependencies for all 450 theorems. These results establish the registered finite classifications; they do not stand in for the remaining source closure arguments, control records or admission procedures.

On the annotated registration domain, passing also entails empty axiom and free-parameter lists and no missing dependencies under the source authority lookup. The typed Root registration serializes identically to its source record. Registration diagnostic order is checked on 272 cases.

The admission engine’s forcing gate preserves four ordered requirements: decision identifiers match the generated census in order; exactly one decision survives; every decision provides an explicit reason; every proof identity has canonical SHA-256 syntax. On the source-typed Candidate and CandidateDecision domains, a constructive theorem establishes that an empty violation list implies a survivor list of length one. The original Root decisions have one survivor, while marking both decisions as surviving produces two and is proved to be rejected by this gate. This connects the source tampering premise to the engine’s actual rejection condition.

Closure evidence retains its source scope, exact boundary, minimality and uniqueness flags, proof identity and optional generality identity. Within the source-typed field domains, passing the closure gate requires a nonblank boundary and successful minimality and uniqueness flags; depth-independent closure also requires a canonical SHA-256 generality identity. The control gate retains the required-kind, duplicate-kind, outcome, behavior-text and receipt-identity checks in source order. Passing implies that every supplied control has passed. Root closure with minimality removed or with its generality identity absent, and Root controls with failed status flags, are constructively proved to be rejected. These are engine-gate properties; they remain distinct from proving the complete operational-boundary semantics.

The candidate census retains the source generation rule, grammar boundary, signed expected cardinality, completeness identity and ordered candidate records. The census gate preserves the eight source checks and their diagnostic order. Passing requires nonblank generation and boundary text, a positive expected cardinality, matching candidate count and no duplicated identifiers. A constructive witness expresses each passing expected cardinality as a natural successor. Root censuses with an incorrect registered count or duplicated candidate are proved rejected. Executable correspondence covers 1,440 census variants, including negative and zero counts, Unicode identifiers, duplicated identifiers, blank forms and malformed identities, together with three direct Root cases.

Parity.lean emits the candidate records and their computed decisions. Each verification invocation rebuilds the current Lean source, generates a declaration audit and executes the current parity program. A read-only artifact comparison checks the complete ordered output against the pinned V3 census and elimination records: 1,714 of 1,714 rows agree on candidate identifier, exact form, decision and reason.

The public formalisation repository is https://github.com/MettaMazza/SFT-v4-LEAN-VERIFIED.

Canonical JSON uses the source ASCII-escaping, compact-separator and sorted-key rules on the Root evidence domain. SHA-256 is implemented constructively with its fixed algorithm constants. All 824 audited explicit definitions and theorems have no axiom dependencies. Sixteen byte-message checks and ten JSON checks agree with Python standard-library results; these execution checks are separate from a general proof of algorithm correspondence, which remains outstanding. The fixed Root boundary is ASCII, so its lowercase and substring operations preserve the source predicate exactly. These host operations introduce no free scientific parameters.

The typed forcing-gate implementation is additionally checked on 384 combinations of identifier order and cardinality, survivor flags, reason content and hash syntax. The Unicode whitespace predicate agrees with the host language over all 1,114,112 code points.

Closure/control correspondence is checked on 432 closure variants, 576 control variants and five direct Root cases. The typed control adapter has a native equality proof preserving the serialized source control fields.

The four Fold control outputs, including receipt hashes, and the seven literal closure fields agree with the pinned source. Assembly execution checks cover depths zero through six, four arbitrary prior-word inputs and six signed host indices; the native completeness, uniqueness and return proofs apply to every finite depth. The source literal boundary text predicate is preserved separately from the native exclusion of words containing labels outside the held pair.

Fresh execution comparisons cover 9,240 proposed-support/source-relation combinations and 385 finite-set inputs. The former reach 144 grammar classes; the complete 192-form assembly grammar is verified separately. All four assembly control records, receipt hashes and seven closure fields agree with the pinned V3 artifacts.

Complete Assembly program outputs agree with the pinned source registration, census, all 192 identifier lookup decisions, closure and four controls, including their hashes. An unregistered identifier is rejected.

The machine-readable correspondence lists the V3 source function and source hash for every scientific declaration. The comparison script does not execute or import the V3 scientific modules and is not part of the Lean model.
