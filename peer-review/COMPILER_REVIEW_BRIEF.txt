2. Runtime and certificates.
Runtime retains an ordered input support, source and target expression maps, and an observation function on a result plus its event list. checked applies the exact observation certificate.
https://github.com/MettaMazza/SFT-v4-LEAN-VERIFIED/blob/21cdf559922fb057f8560f42d056ffa035329eb3/SFT/Computation/ProgramEquivalence.lean
SFT.Computation.ProgramEquivalence.exactCertificate states that the Boolean certificate is true iff the two executions have equal observations for every member of the supplied support. certificateSound and certificateComplete prove the two directions by induction on that support.
SFT.Computation.ProgramEquivalence.completeExecutions retains both execution derivations and the observations obtained from them. distinguishingInput extracts a differing input from a failed certificate; completeDistinctions and disjointDistinctions account for the equal/different rows.
SFT.Computation.CompilationRelation.failureRejected excludes a failed row from Verified.

3. The composition step.
SFT.Computation.CompilationRelation.serialCorrespondence takes:
- first.inputs = second.inputs;
- first.targetExpression middle = second.sourceExpression middle;
- first.observe = second.observe;
- successful first and second row checks.
Its proof obtains both equivalences through checkedMeaning, rewrites the second using those three equalities, and applies SFT.Computation.ProgramEquivalence.composition. At each admitted input, the intermediate observation is the same term in both equalities.
The paper gives the identity/successor argument for finite chains. The module also provides identityCorrespondence, inputSuccessor and observationSuccessor; the latter retains ledger concatenation when the input support is extended.

TRY THE EXAMPLE BEFORE READING THE PROOF
A. Exact results.
For e0=x+1/3, e1=1/3+x and e2=x+(1/6+1/6), calculate all three results at x=2/3 and x=1/2. Then replace e2 by x+1/2. Which input distinguishes it from e0?

B. Execution records.
The source records an enter and leave event for each join, name and value occurrence. Count events for e0, e1 and e2. Does an equal event count imply an identical event sequence? Does equal result observation require equal event count in this example?

C. Compose two stages.
Write the two equations supplied by successful checks of e0 against e1 and e1 against e2 at a supplied input. Identify the shared term. In serialCorrespondence, locate the equalities for input support, intermediate expression and observation function that make this term identical across the interface.

WORKED ANSWERS
A. At x=2/3 the three results are1; at x=1/2 they are5/6. The replacement x+1/2 returns7/6 and1 respectively, so each of the two inputs distinguishes it from e0 under the paper's exact-result observation.

B. Each of e0 and e1 has a join and two leaves, giving6events. Their name/value leaf order differs. e2 has two joins and three leaves, giving10events. The full records preserve those differences even though the paper's exact-result observation agrees. These are recorded constructor events, not wall-clock timing measurements.

C. For fixed input sigma and observation o, the checks give:
o(run(sigma,e0)) = o(run(sigma,e1))
o(run(sigma,e1)) = o(run(sigma,e2)).
Equality transitivity connects the endpoints. The second equality must use the same input and observation and the same intermediate expression; the three interface equalities in serialCorrespondence establish exactly that identification.

The two inputs illustrate the source operation. The general theorem quantifies over the runtimes and supplied supports in its statement; its proof is not replaced by these examples. These exercises adapt Sections3–4 of the published paper and introduce no new verification results.
