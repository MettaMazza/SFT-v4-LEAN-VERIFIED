Scientific author: Maria Smith
Method: same-assistant editorial and argument review; not independent peer review.

Change examined: direct abstract and conclusion, revised opening, and new section7.11. Existing sections3–7.10, mathematics, source correspondence and38 references are retained. No new Lean declaration or verification run is claimed.

1. Does the occurrence argument assume the truth of the challenge?
No. The proposition concerns the occurrence of the presented challenge. False content and mistaken interpretation still exhibit an occurrence. The instance supplies the evidence; truth of its content is not a premise.

2. Is using the occurrence to support its occurrence circular?
The ground is the actual instance, not the proposition that the conclusion is already justified. The relevant circularity would require evidential support borrowed from the conclusion. Section7.11 distinguishes this from an instance directly establishing a proposition about that instance. No such borrowed premise is introduced by the revision.

3. Does finite termination merely rename an unsupported assertion?
No. The section identifies the positive support at the endpoint and for the continuation: exhibition, exact retention, exhaustive generation, canonical reconstruction and actual proof preservation. The preceding sections supply the intermediate arguments. The result concerns the failure of epistemic exhaustiveness; it does not deny the ordinary distinction between continuing, repeating and terminating paths.

4. Does the argument require the opponent to prove a rival philosophy or prove the author wrong before warrant is established?
No. The positive grounds are supplied before objections are discussed. A further-certification demand must identify what necessary reason is absent in this case; applying it unconditionally would presuppose regress. Unsupported-premise, invalid-inference, circularity, source-mismatch and counterinstance criticisms remain legitimate. Failure of criticism is not used as the positive proof.

5. Does the root automatically warrant every downstream claim?
No. Section7.11 requires the actual constructive continuation and identifies its preservation steps. Existing sections4–6 retain the detailed source constructions, exact theorem hypotheses, grammar exclusions and worked scientific arithmetic. The revision does not infer external physical truth from a bare occurrence or advertise the philosophical conclusion as a new Lean theorem.

6. Does the strengthened conclusion exceed the target formulated in equation1?
The conclusion is that justification can escape all three defective branches. One exhibited W with warrant and the three exclusions contradicts the universal epistemic necessity claim. It does not assert that regress, circularity or unsupported assumptions never occur. The direct conclusion follows from the positive argument rather than from this review's verdict.

Disposition: no defeating inference error identified in the changed argument. The direct conclusion is retained. Existing source and proof evidence is reused unchanged, with no claim of fresh full-corpus verification.
