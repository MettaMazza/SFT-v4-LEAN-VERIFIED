1. Grammar and totality.
SFT.Computation.CompilationRelation.coverageMeaning proves the coverage check equivalent to every member of the input carrier having an allowed target.
SFT.Computation.CompilationRelation.grammarBoundary places each allowed row in the declared source and target carriers.
SFT.Computation.CompilationRelation.verifiedTotal combines coverage with checks of allowed rows.
Read completeCompilerCheck and everyRelationRowChecked alongside these declarations: coverage and observation checks belong to the same relation.

2. Runtime and certificates.
Runtime retains an ordered input support, source and target expression maps, and an observation function on a result plus its event list. checked applies the exact observation certificate.
https://github.com/MettaMazza/SFT-v4-LEAN-VERIFIED/blob/21cdf559922fb057f8560f42d056ffa035329eb3/SFT/Computation/ProgramEquivalence.lean
SFT.Computation.ProgramEquivalence.exactCertificate states that the Boolean certificate is true iff the two executions have equal observations for every member of the supplied support. certificateSound and certificateComplete prove the two directions by induction on that support.
SFT.Computation.ProgramEquivalence.completeExecutions retains both execution derivations and the observations obtained from them. distinguishingInput extracts a differing input from a failed certificate; completeDistinctions and disjointDistinctions account for the equal/different rows.
SFT.Computation.CompilationRelation.failureRejected excludes a failed row from Verified.

3. The composition step.
SFT.Computation.CompilationRelation.serialCorrespondence takes:
- first.inputs = second.inputs;
- first.targetExpression middle = second.sourceExpression middle;
- first.observe = second.observe;
- successful first and second row checks.
Its proof obtains both equivalences through checkedMeaning, rewrites the second using those three equalities, and applies SFT.Computation.ProgramEquivalence.composition. At each admitted input, the intermediate observation is the same term in both equalities.
The paper gives the identity/successor argument for finite chains. The module also provides identityCorrespondence, inputSuccessor and observationSuccessor; the latter retains ledger concatenation when the input support is extended.
