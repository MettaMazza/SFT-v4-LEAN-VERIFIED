Abstract

Smithian Fold Theory establishes that justification can escape infinite regress, circular support and an unsupported stopping point. The counterexample is witnessed occurrence followed by explicit constructive derivation. A presented challenge supplies the occurrence asserted even when its content is false. Its evidential role therefore requires neither the truth of the challenge nor a prior assurance of the observer’s reliability. Complete retention, exhaustive generation, canonical reconstruction and proof-preserving inference then supply the reasons for the successive structural conclusions. The resulting justification terminates, does not borrow its conclusion as a premise, and stops at an exhibited ground rather than an unsupported assertion. This refutes the epistemic exhaustiveness of the Agrippan and Münchhausen trilemma. The paper supplies the full argument, addresses the justification of its criteria and explains why requiring another certification of every exhibited ground would assume the regress requirement under dispute. Existing zero-axiom Lean proofs establish the supporting mathematical constructions; a worked fine-structure derivation exhibits an exact scientific output with zero independently adjustable coefficients. Sextus Empiricus and Hans Albert are addressed directly, with Aristotle, Descartes, foundationalism and Gödel distinguished by their actual positions.

Keywords: Smithian Fold Theory; Münchhausen trilemma; Agrippa; witnessed occurrence; constructive proof; zero axioms; zero free parameters; epistemic justification.

1. The proposition under dispute

SFT supplies a direct counterexample to the epistemic exhaustiveness claim associated with the justification trilemma. The counterexample is one specified object W: an occurrence exhibited in an actual challenge, together with the source-defined construction trace and the proved conclusions developed from it. The endpoint and its constructive continuation are components of W, not interchangeable meanings of the symbol.

W = (e, c0, c1, …, cn; π1, π2, …, πn).                 (1a)

Here e is the exhibited occurrence; c0 records its complete retention; each ci names a particular generated construction or conclusion; and πi is its retained derivation from identified earlier components. The index n names a finite presentation. General results about every generated depth are supplied by their existing induction proofs, not by treating a finite inspection as an infinite census. The paper displays the occurrence, count, Fold, canonical identity and least-generator connections, then the generator, spatial boundary and fine-structure route.

Let J(W) mean that this particular trace ultimately warrants its stated conclusions, including the entitlement to the criteria used in recognizing and deriving them. The proposed grounds are the exhibited occurrence, exact preservation of the declared constructions, and their displayed proofs; the sufficiency of those grounds is argued, not built into J. Let R(W) mean that the warrant requires an uncompleted regress, C(W) that it borrows a conclusion to establish its own warrant, and D(W) that it terminates at an unsupported assertion or criterion. Merely presenting a record does not by definition make D false. The universal target is:

T: for every W, J(W) implies R(W) or C(W) or D(W).        (1)

Counterexample: J(W) and not R(W) and not C(W) and not D(W). (2)

The use of J does not make the counterexample true by definition. Formal correctness and ultimate warrant are not interchangeable predicates. Section 3 gives the warrant claimed for the occurrence; sections 4–6 give the operations, exclusions and proof arguments; section 7 defends why those grounds are offered as ultimate warrant, including the criteria objection. J is philosophical notation for that argument, not an additional Lean axiom or a claim that the philosophical conclusion has already been formalized.

The claim of disproof concerns the epistemic trilemma, in which each alternative defeats ultimate justification. It does not dispute that a dependency path can continue, repeat or terminate. W terminates. The issue is whether its endpoint and preserved constructions are unsupported. Calling an endpoint a presupposition does not settle that issue: a witnessed record, a definition, an established theorem and an unwitnessed assertion have different justificatory roles.

2. Named positions and exact attribution

2.1 Agrippa and Sextus Empiricus

Sextus’s five modes include disagreement, regress, relativity, hypothesis and reciprocity. The hypothesis mode concerns a starting point granted without demonstration; reciprocity concerns support that depends on the conclusion it is meant to establish. Crucially, chapters X–XI grant appearances while distinguishing them from an account of the underlying object. SFT therefore does not oppose Sextus merely by saying that an appearance or challenge occurs. [1]

The point of conflict defended here is the subsequent constructive warrant. SFT claims more than assent to an impression: exact retention, completeness, identity and uniqueness are established by the operations and proofs below. The response to Sextus is to exhibit the account and its derivation, then ask whether an opposed account can preserve those same objects and relations. Section 7 addresses that challenge explicitly; the argument does not attribute a denial of appearances to Sextus.

2.2 Hans Albert and the Münchhausen trilemma

Albert’s modern trilemma concerns ultimate justification through regress, circularity or an unsupported stopping point. The formulation is reproduced by Schmidt-Salomon. Albert’s own reply in the same volume, pp. 128–129, distinguishes having presuppositions from making them dogmas: he rejects the necessity of exempting presuppositions from criticism, while rejecting their justification in the classical sense at issue. These are separate claims. [2,3,23]

SFT’s argument accepts unrestricted criticism and targets the claim of unavoidable justificatory failure, not the practice of criticism. Its position is that the exhibited occurrence and exact constructive trace supply warrant rather than a merely revisable commitment accepted in the absence of warrant. Albert is not being accused of requiring dogmatization. The question is whether his negative conclusion about ultimate justification applies to W, even when its starting occurrence is exhibited and its successive conclusions are proved. Section 7 states the proposed contradiction at that level.

2.3 Aristotle, Descartes and foundationalism

Aristotle’s Posterior Analytics I.3 explicitly rejects the view that all knowledge is demonstrative and treats knowledge of first principles separately. He therefore cannot be cited as simply asserting that every justified belief rests on an arbitrary axiom. [4] Descartes’s Second Meditation uses the attempted doubt to exhibit the thinker’s existence; it is a historical comparison for the form of the occurrence argument, not an opponent disproved merely by that argument. [5] Foundationalism, broadly understood, permits basic justified beliefs that do not obtain their status from other beliefs. A witnessed endpoint can accordingly be classified as a form of foundation without becoming an unsupported stipulation. The target is arbitrary-foundation necessity, not foundationalism as a whole. [6]

2.4 Gödel

Gödel’s 1931 theorem concerns undecidable propositions in specified formal systems satisfying its hypotheses. It is not a theorem that every individual mathematical result requires axioms, and zero-axiom proofs do not refute it. No such refutation is claimed here. This distinction prevents an incorrect identification of incompleteness with the justification trilemma. [7]

3. Witnessed occurrence and the non-arbitrary endpoint

Consider a presented challenge asserting that no occurrence is available. Its content need not be true, sincere or intelligible for its presentation to occur. A forged record, a mistaken denial and an imagined challenge still supply the occurrence of that record, denial or imagining. Conversely, if nothing is presented, no counterexample object has been supplied. The argument retains exactly the occurrence being exhibited; it does not infer that the apparent external object is as represented. [8,9]

Presented challenge e → the presentation of e occurs.     (3)

The witness is not a second proposition whose truth is simply granted. It is the event exhibited in conducting the challenge. To deny that the event has a particular external cause leaves its occurrence untouched. To deny the fidelity of its description also leaves an occurrence of the attempted description or correction. To deny the occurrence of every challenge while conducting one fails on the very instance supplied. These substitutions explain the endpoint’s warrant without borrowing the truth of the disputed content.

The native record-level statement is exact. Root.Derivation.witnessedOccurrence is the presentation field equalling true; acceptanceIffWitnessedOccurrence equates that condition with admission, and admittedRecordCannotBeUnpresented excludes the contrary field for an admitted record. The proof certifies that representation. The philosophical argument above identifies why an actual presented challenge supplies a witness for the representation; a Boolean field detached from a witnessed presentation is not being offered as a truth oracle. [9]

3.1 Why the warrant is not an arbitrary stopping rule

The distinction is operationally testable within the proposed challenge. An unsupported assertion can be replaced by its denial without necessarily exhibiting what the assertion asserts. By contrast, a presented denial of all occurrence supplies an instance of occurrence. The endpoint is selected by what remains exhibited under the attempted rejection, not by the author’s authority, a desired physical answer or a decision to exempt a proposition from criticism. The attempted criticism is admitted and inspected; its occurrence is retained even when its content is rejected.

This establishes the paper’s ground for J at the root: a witness whose availability does not depend on the truth of its descriptive content. It is not a proof of all claims one might attach to that event. The stronger results in W obtain their warrant from separate explicit derivations. The next sections show those derivations rather than transferring the root’s status to every subsequent assertion by association.

3.2 How warrant passes to the constructions

At each extension, the question is exact: what object is constructed, what operations retain or change it, and what conclusion follows from them? For count, every position is reached by base, prior or terminal, and the completeness proof handles exactly those cases. For canonical forms, equal traces force equal constructors and equal child traces. For primitive maps, the normalization fixes the admitted carrier before the generating behavior is classified. For the numerical route, the dimensions, covers, typed operations and terminal stage are fixed before evaluation. Those arguments are given below with their existing declarations.

A declaration with hypotheses warrants its quantified implication; a specialized result must also supply the corresponding witnesses. This is why the counterexample is W with its source-bound trace, rather than a list of theorem names. A proof of an implication does not install its antecedent as an axiom. The paper claims justification for the propositions actually established by those constructions. It does not obtain additional propositions by silently dropping their quantified domains or antecedents.

4.1 One and complete count traces

SFT’s One retains the complete admitted occurrence without omission or extraneous material. The original product distinguishes no retention, proper retention and complete retention, each with or without extra material. The exclusion reasons are tied to the sole admitted dependency. No retention supplies no retained root. Proper retention omits part of what the record claims to retain completely. Extra material introduces something not supplied by that dependency. Complete retention with no extra material preserves exactly the admitted occurrence. The six-class product is exhaustive for these two source questions; its acceptance equivalence and explicit rejection proofs establish the surviving structural One. Count extends that retained object by keeping every prior occurrence once and appending one fresh occurrence, which is the original base/successor rule rather than an imported completed infinity. Trace ::= base | extend(Trace).                           (4)

C(base) = [base].

C(extend(t)) = map(prior, C(t)) ++ [terminal].             (5)

Completeness follows by induction on a position. At base, its only position occurs in the singleton. An old successor position is the prior-image of a position retained by the induction hypothesis. The terminal position occurs in the appended singleton. Once-only retention follows because prior is injective and terminal is not a prior-image. Thus C(t) is nonempty, contains every generated position and has no duplicates for every generated t. [11]

Uniqueness is stronger than checking these properties on a finite sample. FollowsCompleteGeneration records the exact base and successor clauses. At base the record is fixed. At successor the induction hypothesis fixes the previous list, and equation (5) fixes the extension. Therefore every list following the production clauses equals C(t). The first nonidentity count trace is extend(base). Every nonidentity trace contains that stage as a prefix. The prefixes of the first stage are only base and the first stage itself; base is identity. Hence the first stage is minimal, and any minimal nonidentity stage must equal it. FoldDerivation.firstIsMinimal and minimalNonidentityUnique give the argument for all generated traces. Its two positions are distinct and exhaust its position domain. [12]

Generated forms retain One or an ordered Fold of two child forms. Their canonical trace records the constructor, both held-child labels, both recursively constructed child traces and the return. Equality of canonical traces forces equality of constructor tags. For Fold, it forces equality of both child traces; the two induction hypotheses force equality of the children. FormIdentity.traceInjective therefore proves equality of forms, and equivalenceIffIdentity identifies trace equivalence with exact generated identity. No borrowed tree-isomorphism criterion is needed in this proof. [13]

4.3 Primitive normalization and the unique least generating word

The four primitives are the normal forms of the original total, parameter-free self-map grammar, not four unexplained contestants. The source starts from the exact positive part x and the complete One, with product, self-junction, complete-One cast and ordered composition. At normalized primitive cost through two source occurrences, its retained total self-map forms are x, x·x, One and cast(x joined x). This is the original source domain; the proof does not substitute the unrestricted set of every imaginable function. [24,28]

The reductions are explicit. Product with One on either side returns x: productRightOne and productLeftOne prove equality of the normalized exact values. Self-product is square by selfProductIsSquare. Self-junction followed by cast is Fold by selfJunctionCastIsFold. A closed positive whole casts to One: closedWholeCastsOne uses the complete-turn cast result, not a new numerical coefficient. Thus these expression variants do not create distinct primitives. [24]

The exclusions are also explicit. Raw self-junction is not a total self-map of the held part domain: at One its joined numerator exceeds its denominator. rawSelfJunctionNotTotal proves that failure using the exhibited input. Guarded Take cannot be a total primitive because Take(x,x) is rejected by the strict-larger requirement; takeSelfRejected and guardedRemovalNotTotal supply the countercase. An operation excluded for failing the declared total domain cannot enter as an alternative total primitive. Together the source normalization and these existing reduction and exclusion proofs explain the four-map carrier used in the least-word theorem. [24,28]

The source primitive carrier has identity, square, constant-One and Fold. WordGenerates requires both a noninjective action and a nonstatic positive return. Identity and square fail the noninjectivity requirement. Constant-One sends a positive iterate to One; a returned value must therefore be One, where the action is static. Fold supplies both a noninjective witness and a nonstatic return. Thus a singleton generates exactly when it is Fold. [14]

The underlying Part carrier consists of exact positive rational parts through One. For Fold, the existing witnesses are explicit: one-half and One are distinct and both Fold to One; one-third Folds to two-thirds and two-thirds Folds back to one-third, with those two states unequal. PrimitiveMaps.foldNoninjective and foldNonstaticReturn supply these results. Square injectivity is proved by cross-multiplying the normalized rational equality, obtaining equality of positive natural squares, using their strict monotonicity, then reconstructing equal reduced parts. Constant-One’s positive iterates equal One, so a returned point must be its static fixed point. These arguments discharge the two components of WordGenerates without assuming the final classification. [14,24]

Every nonempty word has length at least one, while [Fold] generates and has length one. Any least generating word must consequently have length one, and the singleton classification fixes it as [Fold]. PrimitiveMaps.leastGeneratorIff proves:

LeastGenerator(word)  ↔  word = [Fold].                  (6)

This is a proof over arbitrary finite words in the source primitive carrier, not an extrapolation from a list of short examples. The carrier and generation predicate are stated so that the uniqueness claim retains its exact mathematical content. [14]

6. Exact meaning of zero axioms and zero free parameters

An axiom, in the present formal evidence, is a proposition introduced without its proof. A definition specifies a constructed object; a proved theorem supplies a proof term; a universally quantified implication proves a relation between its arguments. These are different roles. Lean reports the transitive axiom dependencies of declarations. The retained audit explicitly reports no axiom dependencies for the least-generator, canonical-identity, generated-collection and proof-replay results used here. That is a positive property of those proofs, not an assertion that no notation or inference occurs. [17,18]

In particular, LogicInference.checkedProofSound quantifies over an arbitrary property P of proposition records. It proves that an accepted trace preserves P when the starting records satisfy P and the registered transitions preserve P. It does not declare those antecedents as global axioms. Treating the arguments of this general implication as independent scientific assumptions is a category error. The upstream constructions are not derived from the claim that every arbitrary property is preserved. [19]

Sound(R,P) and Holds(A,P) and Trace(R,A,s)

                 → Holds(afterSteps(A,s),P).             (11)

The proof of (11) is induction on the accepted trace: an old result retains its established property; a new result obtains it through the particular registered transition and the properties of its premises. The checker-to-trace equivalence is itself proved by induction. The theorem’s exact quantified statement explains its arguments without inventing hidden axioms or pretending that all arbitrary interpretations are forced. [19]

Zero free scientific parameters means that the asserted output has no coefficient left independently adjustable within its stated construction. It does not mean that every theorem has no variables. Equation (6) quantifies over words while fixing the unique least word; equations (7)–(10) exhibit a fixed numerical route. Domains, witnesses, generated depth and experimental records must be identified by their actual roles instead of being counted indiscriminately as free parameters.

7. Direct disproof argument against the epistemic trilemma

7.1 The four obligations for the same object W

The witness and the proofs above support the four conjuncts of (2) for one W. First, the presented challenge exhibits e; the stated source constructions and their proof terms warrant their actual conclusions. That supplies the argument for J(W). Second, each exhibited finite construction and proof has a completed predecessor trace; general depth is covered by induction rather than an unperformed infinite traversal. W therefore does not obtain its warrant from an uncompleted regress. Third, a later scientific result is not used to justify the occurrence or the earlier grammar: the fine-structure target opens after the construction is sealed. The source-specific proofs proceed from preceding objects and witnesses. Fourth, the endpoint is exhibited and the extensions are reconstructed, not granted without those grounds. Thus the argument supplies not R(W), not C(W) and not D(W).

Applying T to this same W yields R(W) or C(W) or D(W), contradicting those exclusions. This is the direct disproof defended here. The substantive evidence for its premises is the displayed witness and existing derivations; the conclusion does not rest on calling a Boolean checker philosophical justification. The operational warrant is defended below against the opponents’ actual distinctions.

7.2 Response to Sextus: the account as a construction

Granting occurrence alone is not the point of disagreement. The account now offered is constituted by explicit operations whose retained results can be examined. Consider canonical identity: any proposed distinct forms with the same canonical trace must have the same constructor and child traces; induction forces the children to coincide. The opposed assertion fails on the very domain and operations it challenges. Count completeness has an analogous exhaustive base/prior/terminal argument. These are reasons for accepting exact structural propositions, not merely reports that an impression seems convincing.

The sceptical demand for an opposed account must therefore specify the same proposition and carrier. An objection changing the constructor, dropping a held child, altering a return or substituting an outside physical interpretation challenges a different statement. An objection retaining them must confront the existing proof steps. Suspension as an attitude remains possible, but its availability is not a proof that the supplied justification is one of the trilemma’s defective forms. The argument claims warranted structural conclusions and then the stated source-model consequence; it does not infer the nature of an external object from a bare appearance.

7.3 Response to Albert: criticism and presupposition are not defeated warrant

SFT does not claim that reasoning occurs in a cognitive vacuum. It uses occurrence, identifiable constructions, source operations and proof terms. Calling these presuppositions in a broad sense does not establish that they are unproved scientific axioms. The relevant question is whether their roles are witnessed, constructed or proved in W, or whether an assertion is simply granted. The explicit chain answers that question component by component. No argument from “there are presuppositions” to “this chain lacks justification” follows without examining those components.

Albert’s permission to criticize every presupposition is accepted here. The occurrence argument permits the challenge and inspects what it exhibits; the construction proofs permit proposed errors and expose their exact obligations. Criticizability is not the same property as lack of warrant, just as the possibility of proposing a counterexample is not a counterexample. An error in a particular proof would defeat that proof; a demonstrated translation mismatch would defeat that correspondence. Neither is supplied by a general demand to regard every proof as provisional. The claim of disproof concerns the universal absence of a terminating justified route, not an immunity from examination.

7.4 What the disproof does and does not identify

If the third branch means merely a terminating path, W belongs to it; no fourth graph shape is claimed. If it means an unsupported terminus, the exhibition and reconstruction are the offered counterexample. The claim is local in its exhibited instance and universal in its logical consequence: one successful W suffices to refute a claim that every W must fail in one of those ways. The argument does not require justification of every possible belief. It does require that this occurrence and these constructive consequences actually have the warrant defended above. That is the point Albert’s objection to ultimate justification and the sceptical challenge must confront.

The philosophical step remains visible: the exhibited witness and exact reconstructed consequences count as warrant for the propositions they establish. This is defended through the challenge, the explicit operations and the exclusion arguments, rather than asserted as an imported axiom. The Lean corpus verifies the mathematical statements in this route. It does not contain a separate theorem formalizing Albert’s or Sextus’s entire epistemology. The paper’s disproof is a philosophical argument with those formal premises, and must be assessed at both levels.

7.5 Ultimate justification: the criterion itself is at issue

Albert’s objection reaches beyond the reliability of a particular checker: criteria themselves, including consistency, lack justification in the classical sense he contests. [23, pp. 128–129] A reply that merely supplies additional successful checks misses that objection. SFT’s answer is to expose what each criterion preserves and why its failure changes the claim under examination. Its proposed ground is the particular exhibited distinction and construction, not a general premise that human recognition, a proof assistant or every inference is reliable. The following argument addresses that ground directly.

Begin with the presented challenge e. The proposition asserted is occurrence of e, not the truth of e’s content. If e is a mistaken judgment, the mistaken judgment occurs; if it is a deceptive appearance, that appearance occurs. Reclassifying its content therefore does not remove the occurrence asserted. This is the positive exhibition offered as the terminating warrant. No inference from a reliable observer to an external object is needed for that proposition. Root.Derivation.witnessedOccurrence records presentation at the source boundary; it does not certify the truth of arbitrary contents by assigning a Boolean. The philosophical exhibition and its exact formal record have different roles. [8,9]

7.6 Identity, opposition and inference: the actual criteria

Identity. The object carried through a challenge must remain the object challenged. In the generated form domain, SFT makes that requirement explicit rather than appealing to a general faculty of recognition. A One trace contains its One tag. A Fold trace contains its Fold tag, its two ordered child traces and the return marker. Equal traces cannot mix the One and Fold cases because their first tags differ. In the Fold–Fold case, equality of the traces gives equality of the first child traces and equality of the second child traces; the induction hypotheses give equality of each child, hence of the forms. This is the proof body of FormIdentity.traceInjective. The reverse direction follows by applying canonicalTrace to equal forms; equivalenceIffIdentity therefore identifies trace equality with form identity. [13]

canonicalTrace(p) = canonicalTrace(q) if and only if p = q.

This is more than a decision to prefer one naming convention. On these constructors, retaining the complete trace excludes two different generated objects sharing that trace. An attempted objection that erases an ordered child or changes a tag no longer retains the trace to which the theorem refers. The positive warrant is the displayed decomposition and reconstruction. Requiring the same object throughout is constitutive of this exact claim; it is not a fitted scientific coefficient or an assertion of universal cognitive infallibility.

Opposition. LogicForms represents a proposition by a nonempty canonical form and one of two held orientations. Denial preserves the form and exchanges those orientations. For held, flip returns complementaryHeld; for complementaryHeld, it returns held. Applying flip twice returns the starting orientation, and neither case equals its own flip. Thus denialInvolution, denialPreservesForm and denialDistinct follow by the two exhaustive cases. These are existing proofs of the exact source operation. [30,31]

denied(denied(p)) = p; form(denied(p)) = form(p); denied(p) ≠ p.

7.7 The positive argument for ultimate warrant

The inference requiring defence is from the exhibited occurrence and its exact constructive consequences to J(W), including entitlement to the criteria used. Its defence is not that nobody has found an error. The grounds are supplied positively: the occurrence supplies the initial instance; exact retention supplies the identity of what is being reasoned about; exhaustive construction supplies the alternatives; and the preservation proofs determine which conclusions follow. The question is whether another unsupported assertion is needed to make these particular grounds reasons for their particular conclusions.

At the root, the proposed assertion is that this presented challenge occurs. A deceptive content, a mistaken interpretation and a false conclusion do not remove the presentation of that content, interpretation or conclusion. The occurrence used as evidence is also the instance asserted; no account of an external object mediates the relation. The positive ground is therefore available even in the proposed defeater. This is the original root argument, not an inference from an observer’s general reliability. [8,9]

Retention then answers why a criterion is entitled to govern the next step. One.Derivation.acceptedIffCompleteWithoutAddition separates every source relation into none, proper or complete coverage, crossed with extra or no extra material. None loses the admitted occurrence; proper coverage replaces its whole by a fragment; extra material introduces something not supplied by the occurrence. The complete, no-extra case alone carries exactly what was supplied. [10,32] These exclusions give reasons for the criterion: its alternatives either cease to preserve this subject or introduce a new unsupported dependency. The requirement is not an unexplained preference for a convenient rule. It follows from giving an account of the supplied object without changing or supplementing it.

The same reason is reconstructed at each extension rather than assumed once for every inference. In Count.TraceDerivation, the successor retains each prior position through prior and appends terminal. Every position is a prior position or terminal; prior is injective and terminal is not a prior-image. Thus every position is present exactly once. Removing a position loses completeness; appending a repeated position breaks exact multiplicity. These are the actual conclusions of everyGeneratedPositionRetained, priorEmbeddingInjective, terminalAbsentFromPrevious, removingGeneratedOccurrenceLosesCompleteness and repeatingGeneratedOccurrenceBreaksExactMultiplicity. completeGenerationDeterminesIdentity proves that every trace following the base and successor clauses is the constructed trace. [11] A different answer with the same complete generation therefore cannot be obtained by an unexamined choice.

Canonical identity and inference continue this argument. Equal complete canonical traces reconstruct equal forms by the two constructor cases and induction on their children. For inference, an old result retains its earlier proof; a new result receives the exact transition proof applied to the retained premise proofs. The induction continues through the remaining trace. These are traceInjective and traceSound, whose arguments were displayed above. [13,19] The entitlement to the criterion is supplied by the work it demonstrably performs: it retains this object and carries the proof of this property. Replacing this with a rule name, an institution’s assurance or a feeling of obviousness would remove the offered grounds.

The philosophical conclusion follows from the character of those grounds. The starting instance is exhibited rather than granted. Each extension supplies its determination rather than taking the desired conclusion as a premise. The criteria are vindicated through the distinctions and preservation arguments they implement, rather than certified by an additional unexplained authority. The reason for the conclusion is the demonstrated relation between the retained object and the asserted property. This is the paper’s argument for ultimate warrant: the actual justificatory work terminates in the exhibited instance and its reconstructed consequences, without an unpaid assertion at the terminus. It is not a claim that feeling certain makes a proposition true.

7.8 Recognition errors: what changes and what does not

An objection about recognition must distinguish the existence of a correct ground, a person’s access to it, and a person’s belief that access has succeeded. These are not interchangeable. Let G(W,p) abbreviate that W contains the exhibited instance and the actual construction and preservation proofs of p. Let A(a,W) mean that person a takes an encountered record to be W. G records a relation between this evidence and this conclusion; A records a judgment about encountering the evidence. Neither abbreviation is an added scientific axiom or a replacement definition of J.

A(a,W) does not by itself imply G(W,p). An erroneous A does not imply that an actual G fails to warrant p.

The paper relies on the exhibited grounds and their reconstruction, not on A alone. A reader who overlooks a missing child in a Fold trace has not retained the evidence required by traceInjective. A reader who overlooks a lost count position has not supplied the complete-generation witness. A checker report with a substituted rule is not the AcceptedTrace required by checkedProofSound. Those are genuine possible errors, but each identifies the evidence that has changed or was never supplied. The argument does not claim that everyone encountering a proof understands it, or that a claim of successful inspection certifies itself. It presents the proof so that the justification is available in its actual steps.

Now hold the occurrence, constructions, exact statements and actual proof steps fixed. A different reader’s confidence or misreading does not alter their relation. For count, an alleged omitted generated position is either a prior-image or terminal: in the first case the induction retains it, and in the second the appended singleton retains it. An alleged duplicate either repeats prior-images, contradicting injectivity and the earlier once-only result, or identifies terminal with a prior-image, contradicting their constructor distinction. For canonical identity, an alleged distinct form with the same complete trace is excluded by the tag and ordered-child cases. These are positive exclusions of the relevant alternatives; they are not a presumption that unspecified objections must be false.

Consequently, a general possibility of mistaken recognition does not demonstrate an unsupported terminus in W. To make that objection bear on this argument, one must show either that the supplied ground is not the ground claimed, that a displayed preservation step fails, or that the grounds could all obtain while some further necessary condition of ultimate warrant fails. The third route is a legitimate philosophical objection, but its further condition requires an argument. Simply naming an absent “ultimate entitlement” after granting the grounds is not yet such an argument. Conversely, the author’s positive burden remains section 7.7; failure of this objection alone is not a proof of J.

7.9 The demand to justify the act of justification

The strongest version of the objection adds a further condition: before W can warrant p, the act of recognizing W must itself be certified as successful by a further justified act; that certification in turn needs another. On that condition, a regress follows. But the condition cannot be justified merely by pointing out that people sometimes make mistakes. From some acts being mistaken it does not follow that every exhibited correct ground needs a further proposition certifying the cognizer before it can support its conclusion. The latter is a substantive epistemological requirement, and it is precisely what the terminating construction challenges.

The local reason for rejecting that requirement is that the justificatory relation is already supplied in the exhibited case. For the occurrence, the attempted error still exhibits an instance. For the complete count trace, the supposed missing or duplicate position is excluded by the construction. For canonical identity, the supposed second object with the same trace is excluded by reconstruction. A certification about the person is not a missing premise in any of these proofs. Adding it would answer a different question about that person’s recognition, not supply an omitted constructor or preservation step. One can investigate that question without treating an independent general theory of trustworthy cognition as a premise of the present structural result.

This reply concerns objective justification, including the grounds for the particular criteria, rather than a promise that every subject will feel certain or recognize every proof correctly. The claim is still ultimate: the warrant for these conclusions has no unresolved dependency on another unsupported assertion. It is not lowered to successful prediction, useful practice or general agreement. The question whether a person possesses or understands the warrant remains meaningful, but a failure of possession is not a defect in the warrant offered. The paper exhibits and explains the warrant rather than inferring knowledge from the mere existence of an unread proof.

Albert’s challenge to criteria is therefore met at the criteria: exact identity is reconstructed, opposition is exhibited in the two orientations, and inferential preservation is demonstrated through the full trace. His rejection of compulsory dogmatization is not disputed. [23] Neither the permission to challenge these results nor the fact that a challenge can be formulated supplies the missing argument that their grounds fail. A proof may remain open to examination without acquiring an unsupported premise. This answers the ultimate-justification objection by rejecting its proposed necessity of an additional certification, not by declaring the existing criteria immune from criticism.

7.10 Symmetric evidential burdens and the disproof

The author must provide the witness, the complete constructive arguments, the grounds for their criteria and the inference to J. A critic need not prove an entire rival philosophy or furnish an experimental counterexample. A critic can defeat the argument by identifying an unsupported premise, a circular dependency, an invalid inference, a source mismatch or a counterinstance; the criticism must show how the identified defect bears on the actual argument. A bare statement of disbelief does not establish a defect. Equally, the absence of an established defect is not the author’s positive proof. The same requirement to give reasons applies to both sides.

In particular, observing that no Lean theorem has the symbol J as its conclusion proves only that the philosophical argument has not been expressed as that Lean theorem. It does not refute an argument made in prose from the existing constructions. Likewise, assigning J the value false in an otherwise unchanged description shows formal independence of an uninterpreted predicate, not that the concrete grounds fail to justify. To be a philosophical countermodel, that assignment must respect the meaning of warrant at issue and explain why the exhibited instance and proof do not supply it. Section 7.7 supplies the positive case for why they do; sections 7.8–7.9 answer the proposed recognition and certification defeaters.

For the same W, the proposed source of warrant is thus exhibited and reconstructible; it has a completed proof trace; no endpoint borrows a later scientific answer; and no unsupported assertion is introduced by the root, retention or preservation arguments. That is the defended conjunction J(W), not R(W), not C(W), not D(W). Substitution into the universal epistemic trilemma yields the contradiction in section 7.1. The result claimed is a direct disproof by a positive constructive counterexample. The mathematics supplies the exact constructions, completeness and exclusions; the argument here supplies their epistemological use. Neither a reviewer’s approval nor a refusal of approval is a premise of the disproof.

7.11 Why the same justification escapes all three branches

Take the actual presented challenge e, and let p be the proposition that this occurrence occurs. The evidence is e itself, not an assertion that e is reliable or that its content is true. If the challenge contains a falsehood, that falsehood is presented; if its interpretation is mistaken, the mistaken interpretation occurs; if it is deceptive, the deception occurs. In every case the suggested defeater retains an instance of p. The ground does not merely accompany a possibly unrelated true conclusion: it is the very instance whose occurrence p asserts. This establishes the initial warrant. [8,9]

Infinite regress is absent. The exhibited instance supplies the ground for p without an unpaid proposition about a further object or a generally reliable observer. Its constructive continuation consists of completed derivations: the base supplies its case, and each successor supplies its own preservation proof from earlier established cases. Generality is furnished by the displayed induction, not by waiting for an infinite list of inspections. The justification therefore does not depend on completing an uncompleted regress. [10,11,13,19]

Circular support is absent. The starting ground is an actual occurrence, not the premise that its occurrence is already justified. An instance establishing a proposition about that instance is not a conclusion borrowed as its own evidential premise. In the continuation, the count proof retains earlier positions and the new terminal; canonical identity reconstructs the ordered children; inference applies the actual preservation proof to the retained premise proofs. None obtains its warrant by assuming the later result it establishes. The scientific target is opened after the derivation is sealed, so agreement with that target cannot supply an earlier premise. [8–13,15,19]

An unsupported endpoint is absent. The root is supported by exhibition. The retention criterion is supported by the exhaustive alternatives: omission loses the object, fragmentation substitutes a proper part, and unforced addition inserts something the ground did not supply. Complete retention without addition alone preserves the stated subject. At the successive constructions, completeness, identity and uniqueness are supported by the actual decomposition and reconstruction arguments. Termination therefore occurs at an available ground and its proved consequences, not at a proposition accepted merely because further questioning was prohibited. [10,11,13,32]

The distinction between a ground and a judgment about that ground prevents a hidden appeal to subjective certainty. Someone can misread the count trace or overlook an erased child; such a mistake does not satisfy the actual completeness or identity obligations. Conversely, the possibility of that mistake does not invalidate a trace that does satisfy them. The warrant rests on the instance and the proof, not on an observer’s confidence or on a report that a checker returned success.

A demand that every exhibited ground must first receive another justified certification cannot be used as a neutral standard against this construction. Applied without exception, that demand already requires the regress whose necessity is under dispute. To defeat the argument, the demand needs a reason why a further certification supplies something missing from this particular ground. The instance already supplies occurrence; the exhaustive count cases already exclude omissions and duplicates; canonical reconstruction already excludes distinct forms with the same complete trace. Merely repeating the demand does not identify an omitted reason. This answer does not infer warrant from a critic’s silence: the positive warrant has been supplied in those steps.

J(W) and not R(W) and not C(W) and not D(W).           (12)

The four statements concern the same W: the exhibited occurrence and the retained constructive continuation. The ground and its derivations establish J(W); their termination, direction of dependence and positive support establish the three negations. If every justification necessarily suffered regress, circularity or an unsupported endpoint, applying that universal statement to W would yield R(W) or C(W) or D(W), contradicting (12). Hence justification can escape all three failures. Their purported inevitability is false. This is the direct disproof of epistemic exhaustiveness; no fourth shape of a dependency graph is needed.

9. Conclusion

SFT establishes that justification can escape all three branches of the epistemic trilemma. Witnessed occurrence supplies a terminating ground: even a deceptive or mistaken challenge instantiates the occurrence asserted. The ground is not the conclusion presumed as a premise, and it is not an unsupported assumption. The subsequent count, canonical-identity, least-generator and proof-preservation arguments supply their own constructive reasons. Together they give one justification with no uncompleted regress, no circular evidential borrowing and no unsupported endpoint. A universal claim that every justification must suffer one of those defects is therefore false.

The reason for this conclusion is the exhibited instance and the complete arguments, not a favourable review or the absence of an objection. Requiring another certification regardless of the ground supplied would assume the disputed regress requirement. A substantive criticism must instead identify an actual unsupported premise, invalid inference, circular dependency, source mismatch or counterinstance. Sextus’s acceptance of appearances and Albert’s criticism of criteria are answered by the explicit constructive continuation and the reasons for its criteria. The result is a non-arbitrary terminating justification, with the scientific example derived through the fixed source operations shown in full.
