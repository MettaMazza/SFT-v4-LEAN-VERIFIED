A SMALL EXAMPLE: TWO CHANGED POSITIONS
The supplied fault order is the length t of a nonempty, duplicate-free fault trace. Its first correcting repetition width is 2t+1. For t=2, encode held as five copies. Changing any two positions leaves three held and two returned, so held has strict majority. Fewer changes also preserve that majority. The source has two labels, held and returned; the symmetric argument recovers returned.

Why does width four fail? Change its first two positions:
  [returned, returned, held, held].
The counts tie. Width three fails with:
  [returned, returned, held].
The changed label has the majority. Width two fails with [returned,held], and width one with [returned]. Each uses at most two changed positions.

These four words are the original source construction at widths 1 through 4, not a selection of arbitrary counterexamples. For general positive width w<=2t, the operation changes the first floor((w+1)/2) positions. That number is at most t, and the remaining held count cannot exceed the returned count.

WHAT THE NATIVE ARGUMENT PROVES
The all-mask correction result and the smaller-width counterexamples are separate obligations. The paper first counts changed and unchanged labels for every mask within the allowance. At width 2t+1, the original count is at least t+1 while the changed count is at most t. It then constructs a failing row for every smaller positive width. Together they establish the first correcting width for this declared repetition and decoder construction.

FOLLOW RECOVERY INTO A CIRCUIT
The source recoverWord operation uses the canonical mask changing the first t positions of each width-(2t+1) block. everySourceWordRecovers proves exact equality for every word whose labels are held or returned, by induction on the word and the two label cases.

recoveryPreservesCircuit takes that successful recovered word, a branch position with its surrounding branches and phase label, a cycle and a gate list. Replacing recovered by source preserves the complete evaluate result. Its proof substitutes the exact word-recovery equality. This includes the evaluator's returned trace or error; read the actual hypotheses and result type.

For the distinct circuit-reversal result, inspect:
  SFT.QuantumComputation.Circuit.circuitInverseAndLedger
It assumes an admitted initial state, valid gates and successful evaluation. It returns the reverse-order backward trace, one predecessor state per gate, and preservation of branch count.
