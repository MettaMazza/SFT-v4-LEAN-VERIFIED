NOTATION KEY FOR SECTIONS 1–2
The paper uses two different records in Section 1. In the raw OrientedStructure record, C is a list of positive counts, A a list of positive-count pairs, and o a held label. In the named comparison record M = (N,C,E,Q,O), C instead denotes element labels. Keep those records distinct when reading the transition.

For the named comparison:
- N: ordered occurrence names (MolecularCarrier.Raw.occurrences).
- C: element labels aligned by position with N (Raw.elements).
- E: pairs of occurrence names recording bonds (Raw.bonds).
- Q: the open-charge-fibre record (Raw.openChargeFibres); MolecularCarrier.admit requires it to be empty.
- O: named orientation rows, a list of (occurrence name, orientation word). wordAt returns the first matching row, or none if no row exists.
- OrientationCell: structural EmptyOne, or a held label with its family and label fields. The labels abbreviated f1 and f2 are fibre-one and fibre-two.
- w: an ordered triple of orientation cells (OrientationWord).
- r: reflectCell, exchanging fibre-one and fibre-two, preserving the label family, fixing EmptyOne and leaving other held labels unchanged.
- Rw: reflectWord, applying r to all three cells. RM: mirrorMolecule, retaining the carrier and reflecting each named orientation row.
- p: a target-name order. Pairing the source occurrence list with p by position gives the map written phi_p.
- P(M,M',p): properMatches. It checks the element and bond requirements through MolecularIsomer.candidateMatches and the orientation requirement through orientationMatches. Both corresponding orientation rows must exist; absent rows do not pass.
- chi(M): chiral M, the Boolean negation of hasProperSuperposition M (mirrorMolecule M). The latter checks equal composition signatures and searches the complete generated target orders.
- Perm(p,N): p is a permutation of N. In Lean the relation is List.Perm p N. The list of generated orders is arrangementsCore N.

This key expands the existing notation; it does not change the source domain or the hypotheses of any theorem. Molecular admission and the nonempty/distinct-support hypotheses of the quantified comparison are stated separately in the paper and declarations.

1. THE QUANTIFIED COMPARISON
chiralityExactlyAllProperMapsFail states, for nonempty distinct occurrence support, that chiral m = true is equivalent to properMatches m (mirrorMolecule m) order = false for every order permuting that support. Its proof uses the preserved composition signature and noSuperpositionExactly. This is a quantified equivalence, not a conclusion inferred from the two-occurrence replay.

The proper comparison retains element identity, both bond-inclusion directions and the orientation comparison. Section 2 specifies these operations; Section 1 distinguishes the raw oriented constructor from the admitted named molecular carrier.

2. WHY THE NEW OCCURRENCE MUST BE FIXED
uniqueBranchLabelForcesFixedName concerns two extended carriers and an element-preserving permutation. The new name is absent from both old supports; each old name word matches its element-word width; the old supports have equal lengths; and the new elemental label is absent from the old target element word.

The proof obtains the image of the fresh occurrence from the zipped map. Permutation membership puts that image either in the old target support or at the new occurrence. In the old-support case, element preservation would put the new label in the old element word, contradicting freshness. The remaining image is the new occurrence itself. Fixing it is derived from the comparison, not imposed on the map census.

3. RESTRICTING THE SUCCESSFUL MAP
uniqueBranchProperRestriction takes a successful proper comparison of the extended carriers. In addition to the preceding shape conditions, it requires distinct old target names and covered old bond endpoints on both sides.

It uses the fixed-name result, fixedLastMappingSplits and fixedBranchMapRestrictsPermutation to obtain an old permutation. The proof then combines:
- alignedBranchElementsRestrict;
- alignedBranchForwardBondsRestrict;
- alignedBranchReverseBondsRestrict;
- alignedBranchOrientationRestricts.

The result is an old permutation whose properMatches comparison is true. Read both bond directions: Section 4.3 explains why an old edge cannot be supplied by a new branch edge containing the fixed fresh occurrence.

4. CHIRALITY CLOSURE
uniqueBranchPreservesChirality assumes a nonempty distinct old occurrence word, matching name/element widths, covered old bond endpoints, a fresh name, a fresh elemental label, and original chirality. Its orientation word and link list are supplied arguments.

The proof turns original chirality into failure of every old proper map, rewrites reflection of the appended branch using molecularBranchReflection, and considers an extended comparison. A true result would yield an old successful map by uniqueBranchProperRestriction, contradicting original chirality.

admittedUniqueBranchPreservesChirality obtains the shape conditions from MolecularCarrier.admit m.carrier = .ok m.carrier through admittedMoleculeShape and MolecularCarrier.allConstituentEndpoints, then applies the closure theorem. Inspect the stated hypotheses and supplied branch arguments when assessing an example.
